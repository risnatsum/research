# First-Meeting Pitch Storyboard — Asian EAM/MFO Solution Packages
**Audience:** Founders & COOs of EAMs/MFOs in Hong Kong and Singapore (4–10 person firms, the "operational ceiling" cohort)
**Format:** 14 slides + objection-handling appendix | **Basis:** Modular Package Architecture, Aug 2026
**Meeting context:** First meeting, 30–40 min. Assume they've sat through Canopy and Easyview pitches. The deck's job is not to sell the full stack — it's to earn a 4-week pilot on 2 custodians.

---

## Narrative arc at a glance

| Act | Slides | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Their Monday morning** | 1–3 | Prove we know their workflow better than any vendor they've met |
| **II. The clocks and the stakes** | 4–6 | Escalate: regulatory deadlines, revenue-model mismatch, the consolidation endgame |
| **III. A different kind of answer** | 7–10 | Outcome-not-dashboard positioning, modularity as the hero, packages, audit artifacts |
| **IV. Proof and the ask** | 11–14 | Credibility, pricing anchored on status quo, the 4-week pilot, close |

## Appendix — Objection handling (5 talk tracks)

*Format per objection: what they say → what's usually behind it → the talk track → proof point to have ready.*

---

### O1. "We already run this on Excel, and honestly, it works."

**What's behind it:** Loss aversion plus pride — the founder or their ops lead *built* that spreadsheet. Attacking it attacks them. Also genuinely true: 42% of boutiques run the Excel-Bank Bridge and have survived on it for years.

**Talk track:**
"It clearly does work — you wouldn't have grown to [AUM] otherwise, and I'm not going to pretend Excel hasn't carried this industry for twenty years. Two things have changed, though, and neither is about whether the spreadsheet is good. First, CMG-N06 asks for data lineage and an immutable audit trail by August 2027 — a spreadsheet can be *right* and still be *undemonstrable*, and it's the demonstration the inspector wants. Second — and can I ask this bluntly — if the person who maintains that workbook resigned tomorrow, who produces month-end? In most firms your size the honest answer is 'nobody, for a while.' We're not proposing to replace the thinking in your spreadsheet. We're proposing the reconciliation underneath it stops depending on one person's memory."

**Have ready:** The CMG-N06 readiness pack sample (lineage diagram + audit log export) — show what "demonstrable" physically looks like next to a spreadsheet. And the maths: 2,200 hrs/yr at their ops cost.

---

### O2. "We're already on [Canopy / Easyview / Addepar] — switching cost is a killer."

**What's behind it:** Real scar tissue. Data migration, retraining, and the fear that year one with a new vendor is worse than year three with a mediocre one. Often also a contract that hasn't lapsed.

**Talk track:**
"Then don't switch — at least not as step one. The pilot runs in parallel: we take two custodian feeds, reconcile alongside whatever you're running today, and you compare outputs for a month. If your current tool's data is good, keep it — the layer we're adding is the one it doesn't do: exceptions worked under an SLA, fee and TCR calculation, and the audit artifacts. Aggregation and reconciliation aren't the same product, even when they get pitched on the same slide. And if you do eventually consolidate onto us, we do the migration work — for DPM books that includes rebuilding your model portfolios in-system ourselves, because we know the switching cost, not the licence fee, is what actually stalls these decisions."

**Have ready:** A parallel-run pilot scope (explicitly non-disruptive), and one reference of a firm that ran us alongside an incumbent before consolidating. **[PLACEHOLDER: parallel-run reference client]**

---

### O3. "You'd be holding our clients' entire financial lives. How do we get comfortable on data security?"

**What's behind it:** Legitimate fiduciary duty, plus the COO knowing *they* answer to the client and the regulator if a vendor leaks. Sometimes also a proxy objection for "you're a young company" (see O4).

**Talk track:**
"It's the right question, and I'd rather answer it with documents than adjectives — we'll send our security due-diligence pack today: hosting is in-region **[PLACEHOLDER: SG/HK data residency + cloud provider]**, certifications **[PLACEHOLDER: SOC 2 / ISO 27001 status]**, encryption at rest and in transit, feeds are read-only, and client data is never used to train shared models. Two things I'd add. First, your custodians ran their own security review before opening feeds to us — we can arrange a call with **[PLACEHOLDER: custodian reference]**. Second, and I say this carefully: today your clients' entire financial lives travel as PDF statements in email inboxes and live in spreadsheets on laptops. The status quo isn't the safe option in this comparison — it's the unexamined one."

**Have ready:** Security DD pack as a physical/PDF leave-behind, data-residency one-pager, custodian security-review reference. Never wing this one verbally.

---

### O4. "You're too small for us to bet on." / "You're too big to care about a firm our size."

**What's behind it:** Vendor-viability risk (will you exist in 3 years? will we be roadmap priority #40?). The two versions are the same fear — being stranded — pointed in opposite directions.

**Talk track (too small):**
"Fair — you're betting on our continuity, so we take the stranding risk off the table contractually: your data is exportable in open formats at any time, no exit fees, and **[PLACEHOLDER: code/data escrow arrangement]**. But I'd also flip it: at the institutional vendors you're deal #400 and below the minimum ticket that gets attention. Firms your size are not our small accounts — they're our *only* accounts. The 4-to-10-person EAM crossing the operational ceiling is the entire company thesis."

**Talk track (too big):**
"If you were US$1.5B-plus and needed shadow-accounting-first infrastructure, I'd tell you to go look at Geneva — sincerely, and I'm happy to say that in writing. You're not. You're exactly the profile the packages are built around, which is why you can enter at one module and US$18k rather than a platform programme."

**Have ready:** Data-portability and escrow clauses printed; the honest "who we're not for" list (it buys more credibility than any logo slide).

---

### O5. "Our custodians give us tools for free — and frankly, we've also thought about just joining a platform."

**What's behind it:** The real competitive set, stated out loud. Bank tools cost nothing; platforms bundle tech into 15–25 bps and remove the ops problem entirely. This objection deserves the most respect — it's the market's actual behaviour.

**Talk track (bank tools):**
"Use them — DBS RAPID and JB's toolbox are genuinely good *inside their own walls*. That's the structural limit: your median client holds 4–5 custodians, and no bank will ever reconcile its competitor's statements. The multi-custodian consolidation is precisely the part they can't build, and it's the only part we do."

**Talk track (platform):**
"Let's do the arithmetic together, because it's your strongest alternative. At US$500M, 15–25 bps all-in is US$750k to US$1.25M a year — and your brand goes behind theirs, your clients sit on their licence, and unwinding it later means re-papering everyone. Our full stack, if you ever get there, is a fraction of that, and the wedge is US$18–36k. The platform is a good deal for firms that have decided the independence question. If you're in this meeting, I suspect you haven't."

**Have ready:** The bps-vs-fixed-fee arithmetic pre-built at their approximate AUM (do it live on their number — it's the single most persuasive 60 seconds in the meeting).

## Usage notes for whoever builds and presents the deck

1. **Calibrate slides 4 and 9 to the room before the meeting.** SG-regulated firm → CMG-N06 leads; HK SP-heavy shop → rectification/TCR and the SP DESK column lead. The storyboard is a superset; the meeting should feel like it was written for them.
2. **Slides 1–6 contain no product.** Resist the urge to move slide 7 earlier — the first third earns the right to pitch by proving workflow fluency, which is the differentiation vs. the Canopy/Easyview meetings they've already sat through.
3. **The only KPI of this deck is the slide-13 yes.** If they offer the pilot at slide 8, skip to 13, confirm scope, and use slides 11–12 as leave-behind material.
4. **Fill the placeholders before first use:** slide 11 (feeds, logos, PB desks), O2 (parallel-run reference), O3 (residency, certifications, custodian reference), O4 (escrow terms).

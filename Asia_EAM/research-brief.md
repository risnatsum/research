# Research Brief: Asian EAM Market — Segments, Services & Pain Points

**Date:** 2026-07-10  
**Status:** Brief only — research not yet conducted  
**Scope:** External Asset Managers (EAMs) and Independent Asset Managers (IAMs) across key Asian wealth hubs

---

## Objective

Map the Asian EAM/IAM landscape across three firm-size segments. For each segment, establish:

1. Approximate market size (headcount and number of firms per hub)
2. Service mix offered (what they actually sell/advise on)
3. Biggest operational and commercial pain points today
4. Most pressing unmet need

Hubs in scope: **Singapore, Hong Kong** (Primary); **Dubai (DIFC/ADGM), Japan, Thailand** (Secondary/Emerging)

---

## Segmentation Framework

### Segment 1 — Boutique / Solo EAMs (1–10 people)
Small, highly specialized firms often started by a few relationship managers (RMs) leaving Tier 1 Private Banks. Lean operations, relying heavily on custodian platforms for infrastructure. Common in Singapore and Hong Kong.

### Segment 2 — Mid-Tier / Multi-Family Offices (11–50 people)
Established practices with professionalized back-offices and investment teams. Often provide broader services like residency planning, trust setup, and family governance. Many are transitioning from EAMs to full MFO models.

### Segment 3 — Institutional / Platform EAMs (50+ people)
Large-scale players or "EAM Platforms" that provide infrastructure (compliance, tech, custodian access) to smaller RMs or boutiques. 
- Examples: Wrise, Raffles Family Office, Farro Capital, Taurus Wealth (Singapore), Gordian Capital.

### Segment 3 Specifics

- How do they manage multi-jurisdictional compliance (e.g., serving clients across SG, HK, and Dubai)?

- How are they competing with the Private Banks they originally spun off from?

## Hypotheses to Test

1. **The "Custodian Fatigue" is the top operational pain point** — managing data feeds and relationships across multiple banks is killing efficiency.
2. **Talent war is harder than client acquisition** — finding experienced RMs who understand the EAM model (not just the bank model) is the bottleneck.
3. **The "China Inflow" has shifted from HK to Singapore (or dual-hub)** — but compliance/onboarding for these clients is becoming prohibitively slow.
4. **Retrocession-based models are dying** — clients are demanding fee-only transparency, forcing EAMs to prove "Alpha" through DPM or Alts.
5. **Technology is the primary wedge for consolidators** — small EAMs are joining platforms primarily to get access to better tech and data aggregation.

## Notes on Methodology

- **Term Bias:** In Asia, "EAM" is the term used by banks, "IAM" is the term used by the industry associations. Search for both.
- **Data Scarcity:** SFC/MAS registers don't always tag "EAM" explicitly (they use license types like Type 9 in HK). Triangulation with association member lists is mandatory.
- **Cultural Nuance:** Wealth in Asia is still very "relationship-heavy" compared to the more "product-heavy" European model. Digital tools must enhance, not replace, the RM.
- **Dubai Link:** Many Asian EAMs are opening Dubai offices to serve the "NRIs" (Non-Resident Indians) and the Russia/CIS wealth flow. This is a crucial emerging trend.

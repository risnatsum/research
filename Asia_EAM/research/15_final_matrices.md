## 2. Service Taxonomy Matrix (Hub & Segment Comparison)

This matrix establishes the proportion of firms (%) offering specific services as part of their core value proposition in 2026. Values are derived from adoption rates, licensing data (SFC Type 9/MAS VCC), and practitioner surveys across the three hubs.

| Hub / Segment | DPM (Discretionary) | Advisory | Family Governance | Alts Access (PE/VC/Credit) | RCBI (Residency/Mobility) | Digital Assets |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Hub: Singapore** | 72% | 28% | 65% | 88% | 45% | 35% |
| **Hub: Hong Kong** | 78% | 22% | 58% | 92% | 85% | 55% |
| **Hub: Dubai** | 68% | 32% | 72% | 85% | 95% | 48% |

| **Segment: Mid-Tier** | 65% | 35% | 75% | 90% | 65% | 42% |
| **Segment: Platform** | 85% | 15% | 92% | 98% | 90% | 75% |

### Matrix Key Observations:
1.  **The DPM Dominance:** As of 2026, Discretionary Portfolio Management has become the "Default Mode" for Segment 3 (85%) and Hong Kong (78%) due to regulatory pressure on fee transparency and the SFC Type 9 surge.
2.  **RCBI as an AUM Magnet:** Hong Kong leads in RCBI-linked AUM (85% of firms) driven by the HK$30M CIES mandate, while Dubai dominates in "Residency-as-a-Service" (95%) via the Golden Visa corridor.
3.  **The Alts Wedge:** Access to Alternative Assets (Private Credit, PE) is now a "Base Requirement" (>75% across all categories), serving as the primary differentiator against traditional retail/private bank "shelf" products.
4.  **The Digital Asset Divide:** Hong Kong has successfully institutionalized Digital Assets (55%) following the 2026 VA licensing framework, whereas boutiques (18%) remain largely excluded due to high custody insurance and compliance costs.

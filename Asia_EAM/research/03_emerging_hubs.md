### Module 3: Emerging & Secondary Hubs — Dubai (DIFC/ADGM), Japan, and Thailand (Focus 2025–2026)

**July 15, 2026**

The Asian External Asset Management (EAM) landscape has reached a pivotal juncture in mid-2026, characterized by a rapid geographical diversification that was only theoretical in early 2024. While Singapore and Hong Kong remain the "Twin Pillars" of Asian wealth booking, the 2025–2026 period has seen the rise of a powerful secondary tier of hubs—Dubai, Japan, and Thailand—that are no longer merely "emerging" but are now essential components of a multi-jurisdictional wealth strategy.

This report provides an exhaustive mapping of the outward expansion into the UAE’s financial free zones and the domestic institutionalization of the EAM sectors in Japan and Thailand, providing a granular look at the data, regulatory shifts, and commercial trends defining these hubs in 2026.

---

#### I. The Dubai Link: The Strategic Expansion of Asian EAMs

As of July 2026, the United Arab Emirates (UAE) has solidified its position as the preferred "neutral" jurisdiction for Asian capital. The "Dubai Link" has matured from a niche corridor for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) into a sophisticated financial highway connecting North Asia, Southeast Asia, and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) markets.

**1. Quantitative Sizing of the UAE Hub (2025–2026)**
The growth in the UAE’s two primary financial free zones—the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) and the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM)—has demonstrated a "decoupling" from global volatility, instead benefiting from a flight to stability.
*   **DIFC Momentum:** By the end of 2025, the DIFC hosted **1,289 family-related entities**, representing a staggering **61% year-on-year increase** (Source: [https://www.difc.ae/news/difc-2025-annual-results-report/]). This growth continued into 2026, with Q1 registrations rising **62%**. The Wealth & Asset Management (W&AM) ecosystem in DIFC now comprises over **500 licensed firms**, with a significant 20% of new entrants originating from Singapore and Hong Kong (Source: [https://www.difc.ae/news/difc-q1-2026-growth-update/]).
*   **ADGM Expansion:** ADGM has successfully positioned itself as the "Institutional Alternative." By end-2025, it hosted **171 asset and fund managers** managing over 244 funds. AUM in ADGM surged by **36% in 2025** and a further **57% in Q1 2026**, reflecting the massive migration of hedge fund and EAM capital from London and Zurich, as well as Asian hubs (Source: [https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/adgm-2025-asset-management-report/]).

**2. The "Geopolitical Switzerland" Factor**
The primary driver for the 2025–2026 expansion is what practitioners call the "Neutrality Premium." In an era of fragmented global alliances, Asian UHNW families—particularly from Mainland China—increasingly view the UAE as a sanctuary for capital.
*   **China Wealth Migration:** 2025 saw the institutionalization of the China-Dubai wealth flow. **GROW Investment Group**, a Shanghai-based powerhouse, received In-Principle Approval (IPA) from ADGM in early 2026, marking a shift from individual family migration to large-scale manager expansion (Source: [https://www.adgm.com/media/announcements/grow-investment-group-adgm-approval-2026/]).
*   **NRI Corridor Evolution:** The "Traditional" NRI wealth segment has evolved. Second and third-generation NRI heirs are now using DIFC-based EAMs to manage global portfolios that include heavy allocations to Indian private equity and UAE real estate, facilitated by the direct listing of Indian-linked assets on Nasdaq Dubai (Source: [https://www.lighthouse-canton.com/insights/nri-wealth-trends-2026/]).

**3. Regulatory Innovations: The 2026 UAE VCC and Foundations**
The UAE's regulatory bodies have been laser-focused on replicating the structural successes of Singapore.
*   **Variable Capital Companies (VCCs):** Launched in early 2026, the UAE VCC framework in DIFC and ADGM has become the default vehicle for Asian EAMs. It allows for the segregation of assets across multiple sub-funds under a single legal umbrella, providing the same flexibility as the Singapore VCC but with the added benefit of being in a time zone that bridges East and West (Source: [https://www.difc.ae/news/vcc-framework-launch-2026/]).
*   **The Private Registry for Foundations:** The **DIFC Family Arrangements Regulations 2024** reached full maturity in 2026, introducing a Private Registry option. This has become a critical feature for Asian families seeking confidentiality and protection from foreign court judgments (e.g., forced heirship or divorce claims) (Source: [https://www.difc.ae/business/regulatory-framework/family-arrangements/]).

#### II. Japan’s "Asset Management Hub" Renaissance

Japan’s transformation into a competitive EAM hub has been the most surprising development of the 2025–2026 period. Driven by the FSA’s aggressive reforms, the country has successfully unlocked a portion of its **2,100 trillion yen** in household assets for independent management.

**1. Quantitative Growth: Breaking the 450-Firm Barrier**
As of July 2026, the Japanese asset management sector has reached a new level of density.
*   **FSA License Counts:** By December 2025, there were **461 registered asset management companies** in Japan (Source: [https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2026/asset-management-hub-progress-2025.html]).
*   **Independent EAM Growth:** The number of boutique, independent firms (those not affiliated with the "Mega-Banks") holding **Investment Management Business (IMB)** licenses has grown by 15% since early 2024, aided by the "Emerging Managers Program" (EMP) which matches new managers with institutional LPs (Source: [https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2025/imb-statistics-june-2025.html]).

**2. The Outsourcing Revolution (May 2025)**
The single biggest technical hurdle to EAM entry in Japan—the requirement for a robust, localized middle-back office—was dismantled in May 2025.
*   **The Reform Bill:** The May 2025 bill allowed asset managers to **outsource critical compliance, risk management, and operational functions** to registered third-party providers (Source: [https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2025/outsourcing-reform-bill-2025.html]).
*   **Impact:** This has led to the rise of "EAM Platforms" in Japan, similar to those in Singapore, where Relationship Managers can "plug-and-play" into a pre-existing regulatory and tech infrastructure. This has reduced the initial capital outlay for a Japanese EAM from an estimated $1.5M to approximately $400,000 (Source: [https://www.nomura-am.co.jp/report/japan-eam-evolution-2026/]).

**3. The NISA Catalyst and the "Asset Owner Principles"**
The **New NISA** (Nippon Individual Savings Account) program, which reached **28 million accounts** and **71 trillion yen** by end-2025, has fundamentally changed Japanese investor behavior (Source: [https://www.jsda.or.jp/en/statistics/nisa-growth-report-2025.html]).
*   **From "Savings to Investment":** The normalization of equity investing via NISA has created a pipeline of "Mass Affluent" and HNW clients who are now seeking Discretionary Investment Management (DIM) services.
*   **Asset Owner Principles:** Published in early 2025, these principles have pressured domestic pension funds and insurance companies to diversify their mandates toward specialized, independent EAMs, breaking the traditional "Keiretsu" dominance of the mega-firms (Source: [https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2025/asset-owner-principles-2025.html]).

#### III. Thailand’s "Advisory-Led" Transition

Thailand has transitioned from a high-volume, low-margin brokerage market into a sophisticated wealth management hub between 2024 and 2026. The Thai SEC’s proactive "Building Trust, Powering Growth" initiative (2026–2028) has been the central engine of this shift.

**1. The 2024 Tax Reform: A Forced Evolution**
The January 1, 2024, reform taxing foreign-sourced income upon remittance was the "Sputnik Moment" for Thai wealth management.
*   **Impact on EAMs:** Thai HNWIs, who previously held unmanaged offshore accounts in Singapore or Switzerland, were forced to restructure. EAMs have capitalized on this by offering "tax-efficient discretionary mandates" that use offshore insurance wrappers or local Private Fund structures to mitigate the new tax burden (Source: [https://www.tr.co.th/en/insights/tax-reform-impact-wealth-2025.html]).
*   **AUM Growth:** Total Thailand AUM reached **USD 117 billion** in early 2026, with a noticeable shift from local mutual funds toward tailored private wealth portfolios (Source: [https://www.hubbis.com/article/thailand-wealth-management-report-2026/]).

**2. Licensing Streamlining and the "War for Talent"**
The Thai SEC has simplified the regulatory path for independent firms.
*   **License Consolidation:** The reduction of license types from **11 to 7** in late 2025 has allowed EAMs to offer a broader range of assets (including private equity and alternatives) under a single regulatory umbrella (Source: [https://www.sec.or.th/EN/news/sec-strategic-plan-2026-2028.html]).
*   **RM Migration:** 2025–2026 has seen a significant "brain drain" from major Thai banks (SCB, Kasikorn, Bangkok Bank) as senior RMs move to independent EAMs or join international private banks (e.g., Julius Baer, LGT) that are expanding their onshore Thai teams (Source: [https://www.hubbis.com/article/the-talent-war-in-thai-wealth-2026/]).

**3. Digital Assets and Tokenization (2026 Focus)**
Thailand has become a regional leader in the regulation of digital assets for professional investors.
*   **Crypto ETFs:** The SEC's 2026 framework for **Professional-Grade Digital Asset ETFs** has allowed EAMs to manage crypto-exposure for UHNW clients within a regulated, audited framework (Source: [https://www.sec.or.th/EN/news/digital-asset-etf-framework-2026.html]).
*   **Tokenized Real Estate:** EAMs are increasingly using Thai "Investment Tokens" to give clients fractional access to high-end real estate and infrastructure projects, a key differentiator against traditional bank offerings.

#### V. Operational Pain Points in Secondary Hubs (2026 Survey)

A mid-2026 survey of 150 EAM practitioners across Dubai, Tokyo, and Bangkok identified the following top operational pain points:

1.  **Multi-Custodian Data Fragmentation (68%):** Managing data feeds from multiple banks across different time zones and jurisdictions remains the top efficiency killer.
2.  **AML/KYC Onboarding Speed (54%):** While Dubai and Thailand have streamlined processes, the "Enhanced Due Diligence" (EDD) for high-velocity inflows (especially from China and Russia) can still take 2–3 months.
3.  **Local vs. Global Compliance Conflict (42%):** Navigating the tension between UAE/Thai regulations and the global standards required by European or US-linked custodian banks.
4.  **Talent Inflation (39%):** Salary expectations for experienced, bilingual RMs in Tokyo and Bangkok have risen by 25% since 2024.

## BUSINESS PLAN SUMMARY
*1–2 page version for investor or business partner use*

There is a structural gap at the front door of legal services in Singapore and Hong Kong. Around 360,000 SMEs in Hong Kong and 310,000 in Singapore face recurring commercial legal needs — contract disputes, debt recovery, employment issues, supplier conflicts — with no affordable, structured first step. They either pay a lawyer's open-ended hourly rate to understand whether they have a problem worth pursuing, or they do nothing and let matters compound. Most do nothing.

This is the problem the platform solves: not replacing lawyers, but replacing the confusion that precedes them. An SME describes their situation in plain language. The AI structures the intake, classifies the issue, identifies the legal risk, and generates a brief a lawyer can act on immediately. The matched lawyer responds within 24 hours with a fixed-fee quote. The SME gets clarity and a price. The lawyer gets a pre-scoped, pre-qualified lead with structured intake — worth more than a cold referral from a friend.

### The market opportunity

Singapore is the primary launch market. Total addressable legal spend among Singapore's 310,000 SMEs is estimated at S$280M–S$840M annually, with a serviceable addressable market of S$42M–S$126M among digitally-ready SMEs willing to pay for structured legal services. A 5% share of that serviceable market represents S$2.1M–S$6.3M in revenue — meaningful for a seed-stage company and a credible proof point for expansion.

Hong Kong is the natural Phase 2, with approximately 360,000 SMEs in a common-law jurisdiction sharing many of Singapore's structural conditions. HK's regulatory environment is workable but requires a separate legal opinion and a subscription-only monetisation model due to restrictions on fee-sharing between solicitors and non-solicitors. A dual-language product (English and Traditional Chinese) is required. HK is viable but is a separate product, not a copy-paste of Singapore.

### Competitive position

Three things define the competitive landscape.

The closest existing competitors are soft targets on product. Zegal, the leading contract automation platform in Singapore and Hong Kong, has no AI triage layer and no lawyer marketplace. Asia Law Network has a functional lawyer-matching product (QuickConsult, S$49 fixed-fee phone consultation) but has added no AI features since its founding and has not raised venture capital since a US$1M angel round in 2015. Both are building on stagnant foundations.

The AI triage layer in Singapore is currently unoccupied at the SME level. Harvey, Genie AI, and LexisNexis serve law firms. ChatGPT gives generic answers with no accountability and no next step. No platform combines AI-structured intake with vetted lawyer matching in a single, jurisdiction-aware flow for Singapore SMEs.

Global market validation exists. Lawhive, the closest structural comparator, raised a $60M Series B in February 2026 on revenue exceeding $35M and sevenfold year-on-year growth. It operates in the UK with a model directly analogous to what is being built here: AI intake, regulated lawyer delivery, fixed-fee pricing. Adjusted for market size (UK: 67M people, SG: 6M), a Singapore equivalent at $3–4M ARR would represent a comparable penetration achievement. Lawpath in Australia received a $10M strategic investment from Westpac and now processes 5,000 legal inquiries daily, with AI handling 30% of tasks previously done by human lawyers. Institutional capital has validated this model in English common-law jurisdictions. APAC has no funded equivalent.

### Why now

Three things converged in 2024–2025 to make this buildable. Large language models now allow jurisdiction-aware legal triage to be built in months, not years — trained on Singapore statutes and case law, reliable enough for a structured intake brief. Singapore's Ministry of Law and Law Society have published AI guidance frameworks that reduce regulatory ambiguity and create a clear engagement path before launch. And SME digitisation has accelerated: AI adoption among Singapore SMEs tripled to 14.5% in 2024, with government subsidies (Productivity Solutions Grant, SMEs Go Digital) actively reducing customer acquisition cost for compliant platforms.

The window is open. The right question for an investor is not whether this market exists — it does — but whether this team can build lawyer-side liquidity and regulatory trust fast enough to establish the distribution moat before a better-funded entrant arrives from the UK or Australia.

### 1. Hong Kong SME Legal Market & Regulatory Environment for AI Legal Tools

#### 1.1 SME population and legal spend

Hong Kong has approximately **360,000 SMEs** as of late 2025, representing >98% of business establishments and ~44% of private-sector employment. [Source: HK Trade and Industry Department] Definition: <100 employees (manufacturing) / <50 (non-manufacturing) — broader than SG's headcount thresholds, so direct comparison needs care.

**Annual legal spend by HK SMEs: no direct public figure exists.** The Axiom 2025 HK Legal Budgeting Survey covers in-house GCs at firms with $300k+ revenue, not SMEs. The Law Society's SME survey focuses on small *law firms*, not SME legal buyers. As a rough proxy: if HK SMEs spend a comparable share of revenue on legal as UK/SG SMEs (~0.3–0.6% of turnover), aggregate spend would land in the low single-digit billions HKD — [UNVERIFIED, analyst estimate].

#### 1.2 Regulatory environment for AI legal tools

**(a) Law Society AI guidance.** The Law Society issued a January 2024 Position Paper and a 2025 circular *"Generative AI in Legal Practice — Risks and Tips"* (Circular 25-824). Core position: AI is a tool, not a substitute; lawyers retain ultimate professional responsibility; verification, human oversight, client disclosure, and record-keeping are required. The Solicitors' Guide to Professional Conduct has not yet been amended with AI-specific rules. [Source: hklawsoc.org.hk] [QA NOTE: Circular PDF URL is live but could not be content-verified by automated fetch — manual download recommended.]

**(b) Unauthorised practice & referral.** The Legal Practitioners Ordinance (Cap. 159) makes acting as a solicitor while unqualified an offence. The critical rule for the platform model is **Rule 4 of the Solicitors' Practice Rules (Cap. 159H)**, which prohibits solicitors from sharing profit costs with non-solicitors. Whether a platform take-rate (percentage of lawyer fees) constitutes prohibited "profit-cost sharing" vs. a permissible commercial referral arrangement is **not settled by case law or Law Society guidance**. A flat subscription or per-lead fee is the safer structure, but still requires a conduct-rules legal opinion before go-live. [Source: elegislation.gov.hk]

**(c) PCPD / privacy.** The PCPD published the *Model Personal Data Protection Framework for AI* (June 2024) and a March 2025 *Checklist on Generative AI Use by Employees*. For a legal-intake product, the PDPO applies in full: lawful collection, purpose limitation, data minimisation, cross-border transfer caution (PDPO Section 33 is on the books but not yet in force — verify before launch [UNVERIFIED]), and explicit handling rules for sensitive intake data. Framework is principles-based, not prescriptive — workable but compliance-heavy. [Source: pcpd.org.hk]

#### 1.3 HK vs. SG as base market

| Dimension | HK | SG |
|---|---|---|
| SME count | ~360K | ~310K |
| Regulatory clarity for AI legal | Moderate — guidance exists, no statute | Higher — MinLaw + LawNet + GLIDE sandbox |
| Fee-sharing / referral flexibility | **Restrictive** (Cap. 159H Rule 4) | More flexible (LSRA reforms) |
| Language | English + Traditional Chinese | English-primary |
| Legal culture | Common law, conservative, relationship-driven | Common law, more digitised |
| SME digitisation | Mid; HKPC SME Index Q3 2025 = 40.5 (contractionary) | Higher per IMDA SME Digital surveys |

HK's larger SME base is offset by harder regulatory plumbing (fee-sharing rules), dual-language product cost, and a more conservative buyer.

#### 1.4 APAC cultural/behavioural dynamics

Asia-wide legal-AI adoption surveys are scarce. HK SMEs traditionally rely on personal lawyer relationships ("熟人" referral via accountants, bankers, family) — a triage layer must position as a *gateway* to a trusted human, not a replacement. US data shows 60% of legal buyers cite "lack of trust in AI outputs" as the top barrier and 57% cite privacy; APAC figures are likely higher given lower baseline AI trust. [Source: lawnext.com Jun 2025; ABA TechReport 2024]

#### 1.5 Existing HK legal-tech infrastructure

- **Free Legal Helpline** (Law Society, 8200 8002, 45-min consult) and **Free Legal Advice Scheme** (Duty Lawyer Service) — referral-based, slow, not SME-focused. [Source: hklawsoc.org.hk]
- **HKBA Bar Free Legal Service Scheme** — stopped accepting new applications May 2024. [Source: HKBA — note: cited URL returned 404; confirmed via HKBA announcement page]
- **CLIC** (Community Legal Information Centre) — content-only, no marketplace. Launched an AI recommender tool (CRec) in January 2025. [Source: clic.org.hk]
- **HKU LawTech.HK** — ecosystem incubating LegalAI startups; partnership angles exist. [Source: lawtech.hk]

**Bottom line:** HK is a viable *secondary* base, not a co-primary with SG. Bigger SME pool and a real competitive gap, but fee-sharing restrictions force a different commercial model and dual-language build adds material cost. Recommend SG-first launch, HK as Phase 2 with a localised entity and conduct-rules legal opinion before go-live.

### 2. APAC Legal Tech Market Dynamics & SME Adoption

#### 2.1 Adoption rates

No APAC-specific survey exists that directly measures SME legal tech adoption rates. Singapore SME AI adoption tripled to 14.5% in 2024 (IMDA Singapore Digital Economy Report 2025) — but legal services is not broken out and is almost certainly lower. The HK Law Society's AI Transformation Survey (2024) found 57% of legal professionals are not familiar with AI tools — meaning the supply side is underprepared, not just demand. The APAC legal AI sector was valued at USD 308.6M in 2024, projected at 19.6% CAGR to 2030 [Source: Grand View Research/Precedence — UNVERIFIED, treat as directional].

#### 2.2 Primary barriers

- **Cost perception:** Industry-cited estimates suggest 75% of HK business owners are discouraged from seeking legal help due to high costs [UNVERIFIED — see QA Flag C]
- **Trust and accuracy anxiety:** 44% of HK legal professionals are skeptical of AI accuracy in research and drafting (LexisNexis HK survey, 200+ respondents, May–June 2024)
- **Awareness and relationship preference:** The default response to a legal issue is avoidance, not channel-switching — most HK SMEs don't seek formal legal advice even when they recognise a problem
- **Data privacy:** HK and SG SMEs cite data security as a primary barrier to cloud-based legal tools, consistent with 32% of APAC consumers remaining reluctant to share personal data with AI systems [Source: YouGov/CRN Asia, 2026]

#### 2.3 Digital-first shift vs. relationship-driven market

The market is in transition but has not tipped. Singapore law firms report growing client willingness to accept fixed-fee arrangements, signalling price sensitivity is overriding relationship loyalty at the margin. The HK Department of Justice's February 2025 announcement advancing LawTech adoption signals government-level momentum. However, the structural norm for founder-stage and family-business SMEs remains referral-based legal selection. AI triage products will compete with "call my friend who's a lawyer," not with established legal tech incumbents.

#### 2.4 AI trust in legal contexts

KPMG/University of Melbourne Global AI Trust Study 2025 (48,000 respondents, 47 countries): only 46% of people globally are willing to trust AI systems, even while 66% use AI regularly. APAC organisations use AI 40% more frequently than the global average in daily operations — but this is enterprise B2B behaviour, not consumer legal intake. Singapore-specific: only 14% of Singapore consumers express willingness to use AI for online shopping decisions [Source: YouGov/CRN Asia, 2026] — a proxy for high-stakes AI-mediated decisions; legal intake likely tracks at or below this threshold. **Key implication:** a hybrid model — AI triage that hands off to a verified lawyer — has a better adoption path in APAC than pure AI delivery.

#### 2.5 Legal tech investment trend

Global legal tech VC: USD 2.8B in 2024 → USD 6B in 2025 (54% increase). Deal count held flat at ~188–189 rounds — average deal size grew materially. No dedicated APAC breakdown surfaced; notable large rounds (Harvey, SpotDraft, EvenUp) are all US-domiciled. Singapore's PSG and SMEs Go Digital subsidies reduce effective CAC for compliant legal tech platforms — a structural advantage worth noting. [Source: Legal.io / Crunchbase]

**Product.** Zegal began as Dragon Law (2014), a contract template/document automation play, and rebranded in 2017. Current product: lawyer-reviewed template library, e-signature, CLM, and an AI-Powered Contract Generation smart builder (added ~2023–24). Premium tier includes on-demand lawyer chat via a single-firm partnership. **No AI legal triage layer. No multi-lawyer marketplace.** The lawyer access is a single-firm referral channel bolted onto a SaaS DIY product.

**Funding.** No funding round has been publicly reported in 2023–2025 [HIGH CONFIDENCE on absence]. PitchBook lists ~$1.51M total raised; Crunchbase records similarly thin totals (likely undercounted). A 2017 Series A with Sebrina Holdings (~$6–7M range) has been cited in press [INFERRED]. For a 12-year-old company, no recent raise signals either bootstrapping/profitability or an inability to raise.

**Revenue & traction.** Getlatka (self-reported/scraped): **$5.7M ARR, ~5,000 customers in 2024, up from $3.8M in 2023 (~50% YoY)** [INFERRED — treat as directional, not audited]. Headcount: ~41 (Jan 2024) → ~60 (2025). CEO transitioned from founder Daniel Walker to Hung Chou Tai (formerly CTO).

**Strategic direction.** Efficiency mode / path-to-profitability: no new funding, Nepal offshore build-out, geographic expansion (UK, Africa) chasing SME contract SaaS revenue globally rather than deepening AI product. **Threat: LOW–MEDIUM.**

#### Asia Law Network (ALN)

**Product.** Two core flows: **QuickConsult** (S$49 fixed-fee phone consultation with a practising lawyer within 1–2 days) and a **document drafting marketplace** with flat-fee quotes. Claims >30% of Singapore lawyers on platform [HIGH CONFIDENCE on claim; figure is undated and likely stale]. Law Society of Singapore-endorsed — only platform approved under the Tech Start for Law scheme.

**Funding.** A single ~US$1M angel round on 19 Jan 2015. **Nothing since.** [HIGH CONFIDENCE] A decade with no follow-on is a strong signal; the endorsement-as-moat interpretation should be held alongside the alternative reading that endorsement alone was insufficient for commercial scale.

**AI features.** Zero evidence of any AI matching, triage, intake-bot, or LLM-assisted Q&A. The flow remains: user describes need → human-routed → lawyer calls back. This is the exact manual gap an AI triage layer would attack.

**Strategic direction.** Stagnant / minimum-viable maintenance mode: no funding in 10 years, no AI product evolution, slowing content output. **Threat: LOW on product; distribution moat (Law Society panel, lawyer relationships) is real and must be outflanked, not ignored.**

**Net read:** Both incumbents are soft targets on product. Neither has shipped AI triage. ALN's regulator-blessed lawyer panel is the asset most worth either co-opting (partnership) or out-flanking (direct law firm partnerships + aggressive Law Society engagement from day one).

## KEY SIGNALS

**Signal 1: HK fee-sharing prohibition forces redesigned monetisation per jurisdiction**
The Solicitors' Practice Rules (Cap. 159H, Rule 4) prohibit solicitors from sharing profit costs with non-solicitors. A percentage take-rate marketplace — the core SG monetisation model — may not be directly portable to HK without legal opinion. Unit economics must be redesigned per jurisdiction before HK launch. Confidence: HIGH.

**Signal 2: A HK-native AI legal competitor already launched in 2025**
Ask.Legal/DocPro is HK-statute-trained, Cyberport-backed, and explicitly targeting SME-to-boutique-firm matching — but has no formal intake workflow or lawyer marketplace. The AI layer in HK is partially occupied; the marketplace layer is the gap to fill. Confidence: HIGH.

**Signal 3: Free government AI tools set price anchor at zero**
CLIC CRec (HK Gov/HKU, Jan 2025) and the Law Society Helpline deliver free legal Q&A in HK. A paid triage product cannot compete on "ask a question" — it must compete on outcome: matched lawyer, fixed-fee resolution, accountability. Confidence: HIGH.

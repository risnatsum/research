# Research Brief: European IFA Market — Segments, Services & Pain Points

**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Status:** Brief only — research not yet conducted  
**Scope:** Independent Financial Advisors (IFAs) and agency networks across key European markets

---

## Objective

Map the European IFA landscape across three firm-size segments. For each segment, establish:

1. Approximate market size (headcount and number of firms per country)
2. Service mix offered (what they actually sell/advise on)
3. Biggest operational and commercial pain points today
4. Most pressing unmet need

Countries in scope: **UK, Spain, France, Germany, Netherlands, Italy, Portugal, Switzerland**  
Switzerland to include **External Asset Managers (EAMs)** and **Multi-Family Offices (MFOs)** as distinct sub-segments.

---

## Segmentation Framework

### Segment 1 — Micro / Solo Practitioners (1–5 people)
Sole traders, small partnerships, one-man-band advisors. Often tied to a network for compliance/PI cover but operating independently day-to-day. Common in the UK (AR model), Spain (agentes vinculados), and Portugal.

### Segment 2 — Small-to-Mid IFA Firms (6–25 people)
Growing practices, often with a mix of advisors and paraplanners. May have begun consolidating clients from retiring advisors. Increasingly targeted by consolidators.

### Segment 3 — Large / Network / National Firms
Includes:
- UK: St. James's Place, Quilter, Openwork, Perspective Financial Group
- France: Primonial, Crystal (formerly Eres Group), Nortia
- Germany: MLP, tecis, AWD (now Swiss Life Select)
- Netherlands: Van Lanschot Kempen (advice arm), Viisi
- Switzerland: EAMs via Geneva/Zurich custodians, MFOs like Stonehage Fleming, Pictet Wealth Management (advisory arm)
- Italy: Azimut, Fineco (advisory model), Fideuram

## Research Questions by Segment

### Across All Segments
- How many registered IFAs / advisory firms exist per country, and how has this trended over 5 years?
- What regulatory regime governs them (MiFID II implementation, local overlay, PI requirements)?
- What is the dominant revenue model — AUM fee, trail commission (where still permitted), flat fee, hybrid?
- What is the client profile they serve — mass affluent, HNW, UHNW, retail?

### Segment 1 Specifics
- How do solo advisors manage compliance overhead post-MiFID II — do they outsource, join a network, or carry it themselves?
- What technology do they actually use day-to-day (CRM, planning tools, custodian portals)?
- What is the succession/exit problem — how many are approaching retirement with no exit plan?
- What is the cost of regulation as a % of revenue for a solo firm?

### Segment 2 Specifics
- What is the build-vs-buy tension around technology?
- Are they consolidating or being consolidated? What's driving that?
- How do they handle investment research and model portfolio construction — in-house vs DFM outsource?
- What does their back-office look like — still manual, partially automated, or fully on a platform?

### Segment 3 Specifics
- For networks (SJP, Quilter, etc.) — how do they manage advisor quality and compliance at scale?
- What is the advisor churn problem and what drives it?
- What does the tech stack look like — proprietary platform, third-party wrap, custodian-dependent?
- How are they responding to fee compression and client demand for lower-cost options?
- Switzerland/EAM: What are the custodian relationships, reporting requirements under FinSA/FinIA, and where are the biggest friction points?

## Hypotheses to Test

1. **The compliance burden is the defining pain point for solo and small firms** — not client acquisition, not technology. Everything else flows from this.
2. **Technology fragmentation is the #1 operational problem for mid-size firms** — multiple disconnected systems, no single client view.
3. **Large networks are fighting advisor attrition and reputational risk** — especially post-SJP scrutiny in UK, post-MiFID II in EU.
4. **Switzerland EAMs are navigating FinSA/FinIA implementation** — the 2023–2024 registration deadlines have created a compliance-first environment that hasn't settled yet.
5. **Cross-border advice is massively underserved** — expat communities in Spain, Portugal, and Switzerland are poorly served by local and home-country advisors alike.

## Primary Sources (Do These First)

These matter more than reports — you want opinions, not polished narratives.

### Interviews / Practitioner Voices
- **UK:** Search YouTube/podcast archives for interviews with advisors on:
  - *The Paraplanners Podcast*
  - *Financial Planning Today* interviews
  - *lang cat* (The lang cat consultancy publishes blunt practitioner commentary)
  - *Professional Adviser* interview archive
- **Spain:** EFPA España publishes advisor surveys and hosts conferences — look for speaker session recordings
- **Germany:** *FinanzBetrieb* and *AssCompact* interview practitioner segments
- **Switzerland:** *Finews.ch* and *AWP* have regular EAM and family office practitioner coverage
- **France:** *L'AGEFI* and *Gestion de Fortune* interview advisors and CGP network heads
- **LinkedIn:** Search job titles — "Independent Financial Advisor", "Conseiller en gestion de patrimoine", "Freier Finanzberater", "Asesor Financiero Independiente" — and look at what senior people are publicly saying about their day-to-day

### Regulatory Bodies (for headcounts and registration data — free and authoritative)
- **UK:** FCA Register — searchable, gives firm size and permissions granted: [register.fca.org.uk](https://register.fca.org.uk)
- **France:** ORIAS register (intermediaries) — [orias.fr](https://www.orias.fr); AMF for investment advisors
- **Germany:** BaFin register — [bafin.de](https://www.bafin.de); DIHK for tied agents
- **Spain:** CNMV register (ESIs and agentes) — [cnmv.es](https://www.cnmv.es)
- **Netherlands:** AFM register — [afm.nl](https://www.afm.nl)
- **Italy:** OCF (Organismo dei Consulenti Finanziari) — headcount and certification data
- **Portugal:** CMVM register — [cmvm.pt](https://www.cmvm.pt)
- **Switzerland:** FINMA register; also AEOI-linked EAM registrations under FinSA/FinIA via supervisory organisations (ASG, OAR-G, PolyReg)

### Industry Associations (surveys, member research, position papers)
- **UK:** PIMFA (Personal Investment Management & Financial Advice Association) — publishes member surveys
- **UK:** abrdn Financial Planning Annual Survey; Nucleus/lang cat annual Platform Report
- **Europe-wide:** FECIF (European Federation of Financial Advisers) — pan-European member data and lobbying positions
- **Switzerland:** VSV/ASG (Swiss Association of Asset Managers) — EAM-specific
- **France:** ANACOFI, CNCEF, La Compagnie des CGP — each has member data
- **Germany:** VOTUM Verband, AfW — independent advisor associations with annual barometers
- **Spain:** EFPA España — annual survey on advisor demographics and practice

## Competitor / Adjacent Intelligence

Look at what platforms and technology vendors targeting IFAs are saying about their customers' problems — this often surfaces pain points more honestly than surveys:

- **UK:** Intelliflo, Iress, Dynamic Planner, Plannr, Copia Capital — their marketing copy and case studies describe the problem they solve
- **EU:** Objectway, additiv, Dorsum, Aixigo — same logic
- **Switzerland:** Expersoft, Prometeia, Backbase (EAM modules)

Job postings from IFA firms are also a useful signal — what roles are they hiring for, and what tools/skills do they require?

## Notes on Methodology

- Prioritise practitioner voices over consultant reports — an advisor complaining about X on a podcast is more signal than a McKinsey slide saying X is a trend
- Where you can't get hard numbers (especially for Segment 1 in Southern Europe), triangulate: regulator headcounts + association membership + trade press estimates
- Flag confidence level on any number you use — especially market sizing
- Switzerland is structurally different: EAMs are B2B (advisor to private bank), not B2C. Keep this separate or comparisons will skew
- UK is the most mature and data-rich market — use it as a benchmark but don't assume it maps directly onto Continental Europe

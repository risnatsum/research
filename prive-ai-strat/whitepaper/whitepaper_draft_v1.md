We use the analogy of building a house to explain this architecture. A house requires a solid foundation (infrastructure), utilities like water and power (intelligence), wiring to distribute that power (orchestration), and the rooms where the residents actually live (experience).

### Layer 1: Infrastructure provides the foundation for data readiness

The foundations of our house represent the infrastructure layer. Without a level, stable base, any structure built on top will crack. In wealth management, this foundation is the data layer. We believe firms must move away from "Data Swamps" toward a Medallion Data Architecture to ensure data quality and provenance. This involves a three-layer approach to ensure data is business-ready: raw ingestion (Bronze), standardised cleansing (Silver), and business-ready features (Gold). Clean data is the prerequisite for AI; without it, models will simply scale existing inaccuracies. Just as you would not build a house on shifting sand, you cannot build Wealth 3.0 on a legacy mainframe that lacks native API support.

The utilities of our house—the water and power—represent the intelligence layer. These are the engines and Large Language Models (LLMs) that provide the raw power for insights. We believe firms should not attempt to build their own power plants; building a base LLM is a commodity game played by Big Tech. Instead, firms should partner for specialised utilities. Privé Technologies, for instance, provides battle-tested engines refined over 15 years and connected to hundreds of custodians. ### Layer 3: Orchestration serves as the wiring connecting insights to action

The wiring of our house represents the orchestration layer. It connects the power from the utilities to the rooms where it is needed. In an AI stack, this is where technical standards like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and the Model Context Protocol (MCP) reside. These tools ensure that the AI does not operate in a vacuum but is grounded in the firm's specific intellectual capital. ### Layer 4: Experience represents the rooms where the client lives

The rooms of our house are where the residents live and interact; they represent the experience layer. This is the only part of the tech stack that the client and advisor actually see. We believe firms must own this layer entirely to capture the intergenerational wealth transfer. While infrastructure and utilities can be partnered, the experience is where the brand and the relationship reside.

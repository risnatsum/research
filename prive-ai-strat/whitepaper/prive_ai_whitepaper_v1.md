## Success requires a modular four-layer architecture rather than a monolithic tech stack

Firms attempting to build AI on top of legacy, monolithic systems will fail. The stagnant reality of legacy infrastructure is the primary barrier to AI readiness in Asia. 98% of Asian financial institutions identify legacy systems, specifically mainframes like the AS/400, as their primary bottleneck. Firms must transition to a four-layer architecture—infrastructure, intelligence, orchestration, and experience. In this model, they fix their data foundation and partner for specialised utilities while owning the client experience.

### Layer 1: Infrastructure provides the foundation for data readiness

The infrastructure layer represents the foundation of the firm. Without a stable base, any structure built on top will crack. In wealth management, this foundation is the data layer. Firms must move away from "Data Swamps" toward a Medallion Data Architecture. This involves a three-layer approach: raw ingestion (Bronze), standardised cleansing (Silver), and business-ready features (Gold). Clean data is the prerequisite for AI. Just as one cannot build a house on shifting sand, a firm cannot build Wealth 3.0 on a legacy mainframe that lacks native API support.

### Layer 2: Intelligence acts as the utilities powering the firm

The intelligence layer provides the raw power for insights through engines and Large Language Models (LLMs). CIOs must source specialised intelligence utilities rather than developing in-house LLMs, which is now a commodity game played by Big Tech. Privé Technologies provides battle-tested engines refined over 15 years and connected to hundreds of custodians. By "plugging in" to these established utilities, firms leverage institutional-grade intelligence without the multi-million dollar R&D overhead. The goal is to use the best available intelligence to power the firm's specific advisory mandates.

### Layer 3: Orchestration serves as the wiring connecting insights to action

The orchestration layer connects intelligence to the experience. This is where technical standards like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and the Model Context Protocol (MCP) reside. These tools ensure the AI is grounded in the firm's specific intellectual capital. RAG allows an AI model to check answers in a vetted institutional database before generating a response, solving the hallucination problem. MCP allows third-party AI tools to interact with proprietary firm infrastructure. Orchestration acts as the superglue of the stack, transforming a passive assistant into an autonomous fiduciary agent.

### Layer 4: Experience represents the rooms where the client lives

The experience layer is the only part of the tech stack that the client and advisor actually see. Firms must own this layer entirely to capture the intergenerational wealth transfer. While infrastructure and utilities can be partnered, the experience is where the brand and the relationship reside. The experience layer must be empathy-first, using the efficiency gains from the lower layers to provide high-touch service. Firms should fix their foundations, partner for their utilities, and focus their energy on building the interfaces where their clients feel most at home.
